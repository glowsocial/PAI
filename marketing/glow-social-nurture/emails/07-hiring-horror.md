 Week 7: The $300/Month Solution That Doesn't Work

**Subject:** "She quit via text on a Tuesday"

**Preview:** Three hires. Three disasters. Same story every time.

Let me tell you about three business owners I know:

**Business owner #1:** Hired a "social media manager" from a job board. Paid $300/month. She quit via text on a Tuesday - no notice, no transition, no explanation. Just gone.

**Business owner #2:** Hired their nephew who "knows Instagram." He posted for two months, then got busy with school. Posts stopped. Nobody told the owner for three weeks.

**Business owner #3:** Hired a freelancer who came highly recommended. The content was fine but completely generic. Could have been for any business. Customers noticed: "This doesn't sound like you."

Here's the pattern:

Hiring someone for social media almost always ends the same way:
- They quit (usually without warning)
- They get "too busy" (and stop without telling you)
- They post generic stuff (that doesn't fit your brand)
- They cost more than expected (when you add up the hidden costs)

And then you're back to square one. Except now you've wasted $500-3,000 and you're even more frustrated than before.

The problem isn't finding the right person.

The problem is that social media for a local business is a terrible job.

It's not enough hours to be a real job, but it's too important to ignore. It requires understanding your business deeply, but you can't pay enough to attract someone who will.

So you get people who are either unqualified or uncommitted. Usually both.

Meanwhile, Starbucks doesn't have this problem. They have full-time people. With benefits. And backups when someone quits.

That's the game. They have people you can't afford. And "just hire someone" isn't actually a solution - it's just a different way to lose.
