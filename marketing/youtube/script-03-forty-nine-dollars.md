### [SCREEN: Simple comparison chart — Agency vs Freelancer vs DIY vs Glow Social]

**[3:00 – 5:30] THE COMPARISON**

> "Option one: hire a social media agency. A typical agency charges anywhere from five hundred to two thousand dollars a month. Some charge more. For that, you get a dedicated manager, strategy calls, custom graphics, maybe some ad management. It's the full-service experience.
>
> And for some businesses, that makes sense. If you're doing half a million in revenue and social media is a major growth channel for you, a good agency is worth it.
>
> But if you're a local business — a roofer, a plumber, a chiropractor — and you just need your social media to not look dead? Two thousand a month is overkill. You don't need a strategy call. You need posts.
>
> Option two: hire a freelance social media manager. That's typically five hundred to a thousand a month. Less than an agency, but you're still paying for a person's time. And you're still doing calls, giving feedback, reviewing drafts over email. It's less expensive, but it's not exactly hands-off.
>
> Option three: do it yourself. This one's free, technically. But let's be honest about the cost. If you spend two hours a week on social media — and that's conservative — and your time is worth fifty dollars an hour, that's four hundred dollars a month. Plus the mental energy. Plus the days where you just don't post because you ran out of ideas.
>
> Option four: Glow Social. Forty-nine dollars a month. Posts are written for your industry. Published automatically. You spend about five minutes a month reviewing them. No calls, no meetings, no emails back and forth."

### [SCREEN: Side-by-side comparison still on screen]

> "Now, I want to be clear — Glow Social is not an agency. You don't get a dedicated strategist. You don't get custom photography. You don't get someone running your ads.
>
> What you get is: your social media looks alive, your Google Business Profile stays updated, and your customers don't think you're closed. For forty-nine dollars.
>
> For most local businesses, that's exactly what they need. Not the premium package. Just the 'make it not look abandoned' package."

### [SCREEN: Back to the pricing page or dashboard]

**[5:30 – 7:00] WHY IT'S SO AFFORDABLE**

> "So the question is: how? How is it forty-nine dollars when everyone else charges hundreds or thousands?
>
> The answer is simple: we're not assigning a person to your account.
>
> Traditional agencies cost a lot because they're paying a person to sit down and think about your business, write your posts, make your graphics, and meet with you. That person costs the agency thirty to fifty dollars an hour. Multiply that by ten or twenty hours a month, and now the agency needs to charge you a thousand just to break even.
>
> Glow Social uses AI to generate your posts. Not generic AI — AI that's been set up with your business information, your industry, your location. But it is AI. And because AI can write twelve posts in minutes instead of hours, the cost is dramatically lower.
>
> That's the whole 'secret.' It's not a bait and switch. It's not a loss leader. It's just a different way of solving the same problem. Instead of paying for a person's time, you're paying for a system that does the same work faster and cheaper.
>
> And the posts are good. They're not 'well, it's AI, so they're okay I guess.' They're actually solid, industry-specific posts that make your business look professional. I showed a bunch of examples in my walkthrough video — I'll link that below."

### [SCREEN: Show a real business's social media with Glow Social posts]

**[7:00 – 8:30] THE REAL QUESTION**

> "So here's what it really comes down to. The question isn't 'is forty-nine dollars a lot?' The question is: 'what is it costing me right now to NOT have active social media?'
>
> Every month your Facebook page sits dead, potential customers are checking it and deciding to call someone else. You'll never know how many. There's no notification that says 'you just lost a customer because your last post was from July.'
>
> It just happens quietly. They Google your type of business, they see your listing, they click over to your Facebook or your Google profile, they see nothing recent, and they move on.
>
> Forty-nine dollars a month to make sure that doesn't happen? That's not a cost. That's probably the best return on investment in your whole marketing budget.
