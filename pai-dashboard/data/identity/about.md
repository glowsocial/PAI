# About Me

I'm Kathleen Celmins. I help local businesses look open online without them lifting a finger.

## Background

16 years of digital marketing experience, starting in 2010. I've done just about everything:

- **Podcasting**: Helped build a personal finance podcast to 50,000 downloads per episode
- **Content agencies**: Built marketing engines, lead magnets, websites for businesses helping people
- **High-ticket coaching**: $10K for 3-month programs building complete sales funnels, ready for ads

Left personal finance because—let's be honest—it's all "spend less, earn more, invest the difference." Wanted to help people who were helping people get in front of their audience.

The high-ticket coaching was fun until my dad died. After that, I didn't want the weight of high-ticket, high-touch anymore. Too much responsibility. Too much of me required.

Came back asking: *What can I do that's not high-ticket, high-touch?*

That's when ChatGPT 3.5 came out. Suddenly things were possible that weren't before. Went the direction of software.

## What I Do

- **Glow Social** — Social media content generation for local businesses. $49/month, 12 posts, zero effort required from them.
- **PAI** — Personal AI infrastructure for my own operations and knowledge management

## How I Work

- Solo operator with Moe handling outreach
- Build systems that don't need me constantly
- Prefer customers over clients (no calls, no hand-holding)
- Use AI extensively—Claude, Fabric patterns, automation everywhere

## What Makes Me Different

I've been on both sides. Did the agency thing. Did the high-ticket thing. Burned out.

Now I'm building something that scales without scaling *me*. The product does the work. I designed it that way on purpose.

Most people in this space are trying to build agencies with teams of 20. I'm trying to build a machine that runs while I'm at my kid's soccer game.

## What I Believe

**Low-ticket ≠ low-end people.**

Someone once tried to talk me back into high-ticket by calling people who buy $50/month products "low-end people." That phrase fired me up.

A roofer who wants their Google profile to not look abandoned isn't "low-end." They're *practical*. They're running a real business, serving their community, and just want something that works without eating their time.

High-ticket coaches love the "low-end people" narrative because it justifies their prices. But it also reveals why high-ticket often feels exploitative.

I'm not building for "low-end" people. I'm building for **busy** people. People who value their time more than they value a "transformation experience."

$49/month to never think about social media again? That's not low-end. That's smart.
